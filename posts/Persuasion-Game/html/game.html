The persuasion game

Based on Glazer & Rubinstein (2004).

The situation

You are applying for a position at a firm. Before the interview, you submit a CV listing your performance scores from two previous jobs — each rated out of 5.

The hiring manager wants to offer you the role only if your combined score is 7 or more out of 10. Anything below that and, in their judgement, you are not the right fit.

The problem

Calling a previous employer takes time. The manager has to review applications, sit on interview panels, and handle day-to-day work. Realistically, they can only make one reference call before the offer deadline.

They know applicants have an incentive to inflate their scores. They also know they can only catch a lie on whichever reference they actually call.

So the manager has to decide: given what you claim, which job do they bother checking? And if your numbers are too low to be worth calling anyone, they will simply pass on your application.

Think before you play

Put yourself in the manager’s shoes for a moment. If an applicant claims a very high score on Job 1, that is the claim most worth verifying — it does the most work toward the threshold. If Job 1 looks weak but Job 2 looks strong, checking Job 2 makes more sense.
